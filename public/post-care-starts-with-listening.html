Good care starts with listening

Sometimes the most useful thing in the room isn’t a new test. It’s the space to be heard.

BJ
Bernadette Jazmine MDGNotes on people, community & care

The space before an answer

A thoughtful conversation begins with room for someone to tell their story. In this fictional practice, the starting point is simple: what brought you here, and what matters most to you today? A person’s priorities give a conversation direction.

A little preparation goes a long way

Before a visit, it can help to write down what you want to discuss and which questions you most want answered. You don’t need a polished explanation. A few words in a notebook can be enough to begin.

Make room for your questions

You should be able to ask what a word means, request a simpler explanation, or say that you need a moment. The visit-preparation template in the guides collection offers a place for those questions.

Leave with a shared understanding

At the end of a conversation, summarize the next steps in your own words and check that you and your care team understand the same plan. This journal explores communication and the experience of care, rather than providing personal medical guidance.
